### Experimental system

- **Typical diameter:** Approximately 8 nm
- **Atom count:** More than 7,000 atoms per relevant nanoparticle
- **Mass:** Greater than 170,000 Da; representative scans centred near 172 kDa
- **Quantum–classical discrimination range:** Approximately 143–197 kDa
- **Interferometer:** Three ultraviolet optical gratings in a Talbot–Lau near-field configuration
- **Grating period:** 133 nm
- **Representative fringe visibility:** Up to V = 0.10 ± 0.01
- **Macroscopicity:** μ = 15.5
- **Primary interpretive result:** The measured visibility followed the quantum model and was clearly distinct from the corresponding classical model in the relevant mass range

### IEH terminology

- **HDCT:** High-dimensional Cognitive Tools
- **Indirect epistemic evidence relevant to HDCT:** Evidence supporting a premise about the limits of unaided intuition or the role of advanced representational tools, without showing that an AI system has formed HDCT.
- **Classical intuition:** Everyday macroscopic expectations of definite position, continuous trajectory, separable objects, and directly visualizable causation.
- **Representational extension:** A formal or instrumental system that allows cognition to model and test states ordinary intuition cannot directly imagine.
- **Operationally accessible reality:** A domain that can be predicted, manipulated, or experimentally tested through formal representations and instruments, even when it is not intuitive.
- **Important distinction:** A quantum state occupying a high-dimensional Hilbert space is not, by itself, evidence that an AI has formed a high-dimensional cognitive tool.

### Evidence-level judgment

The strongest currently defensible classification is:

> A massive sodium nanoparticle can display centre-of-mass interference that is incompatible with the corresponding classical model and consistent with standard quantum mechanics. The experiment shows that physically real, testable behaviour can lie outside ordinary macroscopic intuition while remaining accessible through abstract mathematical representation and precision instruments. The evidence does **not** establish:

> AI has already formed HDCT, human mathematics is obsolete, or quantum mechanics reveals a machine-only domain inaccessible to human understanding.

- construct genuinely new physical representations;
- derive novel predictions from them;
- design discriminating experiments;

## 4. Core IEH Interpretation

### 4.1 Reality does not conform to ordinary intuition

Human intuitive physics was shaped in a narrow macroscopic regime:

- objects are much larger than atoms;
- velocities are far below the speed of light;
- environmental decoherence is strong;
- positions appear definite;
- trajectories appear continuous;
- and causal relations can often be pictured in three-dimensional space.

The present experiment shows that these expectations do not define the full range of physically possible behaviour.

A sodium nanoparticle containing thousands of atoms can require a wave-function description in which its centre-of-mass state extends across classically distinct paths. The experimentally relevant structure is not adequately captured by the image of a tiny billiard ball following one hidden trajectory.

The minimal chain is:

\[
\text{everyday macroscopic experience}
\rightarrow
\text{classical intuition}
\rightarrow
\text{limited representational expectations}
\]

while the experiment requires:

\[
\text{abstract quantum representation}
+
\text{precision instrument}
\rightarrow
\text{testable access to non-intuitive behaviour}
\]

Under IEH, this supports a general premise:

> The information structure of reality is not constrained by the representational habits of the observer.

### 4.2 Intelligibility is not identical to visualizability

The state is difficult to picture in ordinary spatial imagination.

Yet it can be:

- represented mathematically;
- propagated through a formal model;
- compared with a classical alternative;
- linked to experimental parameters;
- and tested through measured fringe visibility.

This suggests a layered distinction:

\[
\text{intuitively visualizable}
\subset
\text{formally representable}
\subseteq
\text{operationally testable}
\]

A phenomenon may fall outside the first set while remaining inside the second and third.

This distinction matters for HDCT because future cognitive tools may expand the second and third sets even when human intuitive visualization does not expand at the same rate.

### 4.3 Scientific tools are extensions of cognitive reach

The experiment depended on a coordinated system of tools:

- a cryogenic source producing large sodium clusters;
- ultrahigh vacuum;
- controlled velocities;
- three stable ultraviolet gratings;
- mass filtering;
- nanometre-scale phase stability;
- quantum and classical propagation models;
- statistical fitting;
- and model comparison.

The observation was therefore not a passive encounter with an obvious object.

It was an engineered extension of what could become observable.

The structural chain is:

\[
\text{new experimental apparatus}
\rightarrow
\text{new controllable state}
\rightarrow
\text{new discriminating measurement}
\rightarrow
\text{new empirical constraint}
\]

HDCT predicts an analogous cognitive chain:

\[
\text{new representational apparatus}
\rightarrow
\text{new operable problem space}
\rightarrow
\text{new discriminating predictions}
\rightarrow
\text{new scientific knowledge}
\]

The analogy is structural, not evidentially identical. The experiment demonstrates the first chain. It does not demonstrate that an AI has completed the second.

### 4.4 The relevant “dimension” is representational, not merely spatial

Quantum mechanics uses state spaces whose formal dimensionality can be extremely large. Composite systems may require descriptions in Hilbert spaces that cannot be mapped into a simple three-dimensional mental picture.

However:

\[
\text{high-dimensional Hilbert space}
\neq
\text{HDCT}
\]

Human physicists already use high-dimensional state spaces.

The HDCT-relevant issue is whether a cognitive system can:

- construct the right representation;
- navigate it efficiently;
- preserve long-range constraints;
- identify observables that distinguish theories;
- and translate the result into experimentally testable consequences.

The present experiment supports the necessity of such representational work. It does not show that the work has become machine-native.

### 4.5 Human mathematics succeeded rather than failed

The experiment did not expose the collapse of existing mathematics.

It relied on human-developed quantum mechanics, including:

- de Broglie matter waves;
- Talbot–Lau interference;
- Wigner–Weyl phase-space representation;
- decoherence modelling;
- Bayesian constraints on macrorealistic modifications;
- and statistical model comparison.

The result therefore supports:

\[
\text{human intuition is limited}
\]

but not:

\[
\text{human mathematics is invalid}
\]

A more precise formulation is:

> Human cognition can exceed its intuitive limits by externalizing reasoning into formal systems and instruments.

This point is central to HDCT. HDCT is not required because all present tools have failed. It becomes relevant because the historical pattern of science is repeated expansion beyond direct intuition, and artificial systems may extend this process further.

### 4.6 The experiment expands a boundary rather than abolishing it

The result pushes quantum interference to a new combination of mass, size, and delocalization.

It does not show that there is no quantum–classical boundary under all conditions.

The experiment found no breakdown associated with mass or size at the tested scale. Future experiments may continue to constrain collapse models or other modifications.

The evidence therefore supports:

\[
\text{previously untested scale}
\rightarrow
\text{standard quantum mechanics remains adequate}
\]

It does not support:

\[
\text{all possible scales and conditions have been resolved}
\]

### 4.7 Why this matters for the HDCT hypothesis

The HDCT hypothesis is partly motivated by a recurring feature of science:

> Reality repeatedly exceeds the representations that arise naturally from human biological experience.

The present experiment supplies a strong example of this pattern.

It shows that:

1. everyday intuition is not a reliable boundary on physical possibility;
2. formal representations can preserve predictive power where intuition fails;
3. instruments can make non-intuitive structures operationally accessible;
4. theory comparison can remain rigorous without ordinary visual imagery;
5. scientific progress often depends on building tools that extend representational reach.

These are epistemic premises relevant to HDCT.

They remain indirect because the experiment was designed, modelled, and interpreted by human researchers using established mathematics.

### 4.1 现实不服从日常直觉

人类的直觉物理形成于一个非常狭窄的宏观环境：

- 对象远大于原子；
- 速度远低于光速；
- 环境退相干很强；
- 位置看起来是确定的；
- 轨迹看起来是连续的；
- 因果关系通常可以在三维空间中想象。

本实验表明，这些预期不能定义物理行为的全部范围。

包含数千个原子的钠纳米颗粒，需要用质心状态跨越经典可区分路径的波函数描述。实验相关结构不能被充分压缩成“一颗小台球沿一条隐藏轨迹运动”的图像。

最低限度的结构链是：

\[
\text{日常宏观经验}
\rightarrow
\text{经典直觉}
\rightarrow
\text{受限的表示预期}
\]

而实验所要求的是：

\[
\text{抽象量子表示}
+
\text{精密仪器}
\rightarrow
\text{进入非直觉但可检验的行为}
\]

在 IEH 框架下，这支持一个一般性前提：

> 现实的信息结构不受观察者既有表示习惯的限制。

### 4.2 可理解不等于可直观想象

这种状态很难用普通空间想象真正呈现。

但它可以被：

- 数学表示；
- 通过形式模型传播；
- 与经典替代模型比较；
- 映射到实验参数；
- 通过条纹可见度进行检验。

因此可以区分：

\[
\text{直觉上可想象}
\subset
\text{形式上可表示}
\subseteq
\text{操作上可检验}
\]

一个现象可以不属于第一集合，却仍然属于第二和第三集合。

这与 HDCT 直接相关，因为未来认知工具可能继续扩大第二和第三集合，而人类直觉想象能力未必同步扩张。

### 4.3 科学工具是认知范围的外部延伸

本实验依赖一个协调工具系统：

- 产生大型钠团簇的低温源；
- 超高真空；
- 受控粒子速度；
- 三组稳定紫外光栅；
- 质量筛选；
- 纳米尺度相位稳定；
- 量子和经典传播模型；
- 统计拟合；
- 模型比较。

因此，这不是一次对明显对象的被动观察，而是对“什么能够被观察”进行工程化扩展。

其结构链是：

\[
\text{新实验装置}
\rightarrow
\text{新可控状态}
\rightarrow
\text{新鉴别性测量}
\rightarrow
\text{新经验约束}
\]

HDCT 所预测的认知链则是：

\[
\text{新表示装置}
\rightarrow
\text{新可操作问题空间}
\rightarrow
\text{新鉴别性预测}
\rightarrow
\text{新科学知识}
\]

二者只有结构类比，不具有相同证据地位。本实验展示了第一条链，没有展示 AI 已经完成第二条链。

### 4.4 相关“维度”是表示维度，而不只是空间维度

量子力学使用的状态空间，其形式维数可能极高。复合系统可能需要在无法压缩为简单三维心理图像的希尔伯特空间中描述。

但是：

\[
\text{高维希尔伯特空间}
\neq
\text{HDCT}
\]

人类物理学家长期以来已经在使用高维状态空间。

真正与 HDCT 有关的问题是，一个认知系统能否：

- 构造正确表示；
- 在其中高效导航；
- 保持长距离约束；
- 找到能够区分理论的可观测量；
- 将结果转化为可检验后果。

本实验支持这种表示工作的必要性，却没有证明它已经成为机器原生能力。

### 4.5 人类数学在这里成功，而不是失败

本实验没有暴露现有数学的崩溃。

它依赖人类建立的量子力学，包括：

- 德布罗意物质波；
- Talbot–Lau 干涉；
- Wigner–Weyl 相空间表示；
- 退相干建模；
- 对宏观实在论修正模型的贝叶斯约束；
- 统计模型比较。

因此它支持：

\[
\text{人类直觉存在边界}
\]

却不支持：

\[
\text{人类数学已经失效}
\]

更准确的表达是：

> 人类认知可以通过把推理外化为形式系统和实验仪器，超越自身直觉边界。

这一点对 HDCT 至关重要。HDCT 并不是因为现有一切工具都已经失败才有必要，而是因为科学史持续表现为超越直接直觉的表示扩张，人工系统可能继续把这一过程推向更远。

### 4.6 实验扩展边界，而不是消灭边界问题

本结果把量子干涉推进到新的质量、尺寸和离域组合。

它不能证明在所有条件下都不存在量子—经典边界。

实验只是在被测试尺度上没有发现与质量或尺寸相关的失效。未来实验仍可继续约束坍缩模型或其他修正。

因此证据支持：

\[
\text{此前未充分检验的尺度}
\rightarrow
\text{标准量子力学仍然充分}
\]

而不支持：

\[
\text{所有可能尺度和条件已经解决}
\]

### 4.7 为什么它与 HDCT 假说有关

HDCT 假说的一项重要动机，是科学反复呈现如下事实：

> 现实不断超出人类生物经验自然产生的表示方式。

本实验为这一模式提供了强有力案例。

它表明：

1. 日常直觉不是物理可能性的可靠边界；
2. 当直觉失效时，形式表示仍可保持预测能力；
3. 仪器能够使非直觉结构在操作上进入现实；
4. 即使缺乏普通视觉图像，理论比较仍可保持严格；
5. 科学进步经常依赖扩大表示范围的工具建设。

这些是与 HDCT 有关的认识论前提。

但这种关联仍然是间接的，因为本实验由人类研究者使用既有数学设计、建模和解释。

## 6. Competing Explanations and Limitations

### 6.1 Classical fringe explanation

Periodic density fringes alone can arise from classical moiré or lensing effects.

The authors addressed this by varying the second grating’s laser power and comparing visibility with both quantum and classical predictions. The observed dependence was consistent with the quantum model and distinct from the classical model in the relevant mass regime.

This alternative is therefore weakened for the reported quantum-discrimination data, but it remains important for understanding why fringe observation alone is insufficient.

### 6.2 Model dependence

The conclusion depends on:

- measured mass and velocity distributions;
- ionization cross-sections;
- optical polarizability;
- alignment;
- vibration control;
- gravitational and rotational phase averaging;
- decoherence estimates;
- and the validity of the theoretical comparison.

The authors report independent support for important input assumptions and apply the same global experimental factor to quantum and classical curves. Nevertheless, model-based inference remains part of the evidential chain.

### 6.3 Higher-mass ambiguity

The apparatus observed high-visibility fringes for particles approaching the megadalton range, but quantum and classical predictions converge in that regime under the existing configuration.

Therefore:

\[
\text{higher mass fringe}
\neq
\text{higher mass verified quantum interference}
\]

The mass record relevant to quantum–classical discrimination should not be inflated by including those ambiguous observations.

### 6.4 “Macroscopicity” is a defined metric

The value μ = 15.5 is not a direct measure of everyday visible size.

It is a formal metric describing how strongly an experiment constrains a specified class of minimal macrorealistic modifications.

Different macroscopicity concepts may rank experiments differently.

### 6.5 The object remains nanoscale

An 8 nm cluster is very large relative to atoms and many previous matter-wave particles, but extremely small relative to ordinary macroscopic objects.

The term “macroscopic” must therefore be understood within the experimental metric and quantum-foundations context.

### 6.6 Decoherence and isolation

The experiment demonstrates quantum behaviour under carefully isolated conditions.

It does not imply that similar superpositions remain observable under ordinary environmental interaction, where collisions, radiation, thermal processes, and uncontrolled coupling rapidly suppress interference.

### 6.7 Established theory rather than new cognitive framework

The result is an impressive extension of known quantum theory into a new experimental regime.

It does not by itself constitute a new representation, a new physical law, or an HDCT-generated paradigm.

### 6.8 Human cognitive scaffolding

The experiment depended on accumulated human knowledge, institutional collaboration, mathematical theory, engineering, and instruments.

Its relevance to HDCT arises from the structure of tool-mediated cognition, not from replacement of the human research system.

### 6.9 Interpretation underdetermination

The observed interference is compatible with standard quantum predictions, but the experiment alone does not decide among major interpretations of quantum mechanics that share those predictions.

### 6.10 Replication status

The primary article reports multiple runs and internal consistency checks. This note does not document an independent replication by another laboratory using a separate apparatus.

### Current evidential rule

The evidence should remain classified as indirect unless future AI systems themselves:

- formulate new quantum representations;
- derive non-obvious experimentally discriminating predictions;
- design feasible apparatus;
- guide data collection;
- and generate results independently verified by physicists.

## 6. 竞争性解释与局限

### 6.1 经典条纹解释

周期密度条纹本身也可能由经典莫尔效应或光学透镜效应产生。

作者通过改变第二组光栅的激光功率，并比较量子与经典模型对可见度的预测来处理这一问题。相关质量区间的实验依赖关系符合量子模型，并与经典模型区分。

因此，经典解释在报告的量子鉴别数据中受到削弱，但它仍说明为什么“看见条纹”本身不足以证明量子传播。

### 6.2 模型依赖

结论依赖：

- 实测质量和速度分布；
- 电离截面；
- 光学极化率；
- 装置对准；
- 振动控制；
- 重力和转动相位平均；
- 退相干估计；
- 理论比较的有效性。

作者对重要输入假设提供了独立支持，并对量子和经典曲线使用相同的全局实验因子。不过，模型推断仍然是证据链的一部分。

### 6.3 更高质量区间的歧义

装置在接近兆道尔顿的粒子中观察到高可见度条纹，但在现有配置下，量子和经典预测在该区间趋于一致。

因此：

\[
\text{更高质量条纹}
\neq
\text{更高质量已验证量子干涉}
\]

不能把这些具有歧义的观察直接加入量子—经典可区分的质量纪录。

### 6.4 “宏观性”是特定定义指标

μ = 15.5 不是日常可见尺寸的直接度量。

它是描述实验能够多强地约束一类最小宏观实在论修正模型的形式指标。

不同宏观性定义可能对实验作出不同排序。

### 6.5 对象仍然是纳米尺度

8 nm 团簇相对于原子和许多此前物质波粒子非常大，但相对于日常宏观对象仍极小。

因此“宏观”应在实验指标和量子基础研究语境中理解。

### 6.6 退相干与隔离条件

实验在精密隔离条件下展示量子行为。

它不表示类似叠加在普通环境相互作用下仍可观察；碰撞、辐射、热过程和不可控耦合会迅速抑制干涉。

### 6.7 既有理论的扩展，而非新认知框架

该结果把已知量子理论推进到新的实验区间。

它本身不构成新表示、新物理定律或由 HDCT 生成的范式。

### 6.8 人类认知支架

实验依赖累积的人类知识、机构合作、数学理论、工程和仪器。

它与 HDCT 的关联来自工具介导认知的结构，而不是人类研究系统已被替代。

### 6.9 解释不唯一

观察到的干涉符合标准量子预测，但实验不能单独裁决共享相同预测的主要量子力学解释。

### 6.10 复现状态

原始论文报告多次实验和内部一致性检查。本笔记没有记录另一实验室使用独立装置完成的复现。

### 当前证据规则

除非未来 AI 系统自身能够：

- 构造新的量子表示；
- 推导非显然且具有实验区分力的预测；
- 设计可实施装置；
- 指导数据采集；
- 并产生经物理学家独立验证的结果，

该事件仍应保持间接证据分级。

## 7. Testable Predictions

The following predictions are independently derived from IEH. They are not claims attributed to the experimental authors.

### P1. Scale-extension prediction

If no intrinsic mass threshold suppresses quantum interference in the currently accessible regime, future experiments with slower and better-isolated particles should distinguish quantum from classical propagation at masses above 1 MDa.

### P2. Biological-complexity prediction

Matter-wave experiments should progressively extend from metal clusters to complex organic and nanobiological objects with comparable masses, provided beam preparation, isolation, coherent manipulation, and detection improve.

### P3. Representation–instrument co-evolution prediction

Major advances at the quantum–classical boundary should increasingly require joint development of:

- improved formal models;
- more stable instruments;
- better parameter estimation;
- and automated experiment control.

Progress should correlate with the integration of representational and physical tools, not with theory or hardware alone.

### P4. Intuition-gap prediction

As experiments access more non-intuitive regimes, the distance between ordinary verbal explanations and the operational mathematical description should grow.

Public metaphors such as “here and there” will become less adequate for the underlying state structure.

### P5. Machine-assisted modelling prediction

AI systems should increasingly assist with:

- high-dimensional parameter search;
- noise and decoherence modelling;
- apparatus optimization;
- anomaly detection;
- and discrimination between competing physical models.

This would be tool assistance, not yet realized HDCT.

### P6. Novel-representation prediction

A stronger HDCT signal would occur if an artificial system develops a new representation that:

- compresses a difficult quantum problem;
- reveals a previously unnoticed invariant or observable;
- generates a falsifiable prediction;
- and is validated experimentally.

### P7. Machine-native experiment-design prediction

Systems approaching HDCT should propose experiments whose logic is not initially obvious to human researchers but can be translated into independently testable protocols.

### P8. Verification-interface prediction

If machine-generated physical reasoning becomes difficult to reconstruct, scientific acceptance will increasingly depend on interfaces that provide:

- formal derivations;
- simulation outputs;
- sensitivity analyses;
- falsification conditions;
- and machine-checkable experimental predictions.

### P9. Classical-model challenge prediction

Future high-mass interference claims will continue to require explicit exclusion of classical moiré, lensing, shadow, vibration, and detection artefacts.

The evidential standard should rise as systems become more complex.

### P10. Decoherence-control prediction

Increasing object complexity will shift the main bottleneck toward:

- source quality;
- internal temperature;
- environmental isolation;
- velocity reduction;
- and control of photon and gas scattering.

### P11. Cross-domain representation prediction

Mature HDCT may connect quantum foundations, control theory, materials science, statistics, and experimental engineering in ways that produce new testable architectures rather than isolated calculations.

### P12. Human–machine complementarity prediction

Before a fully machine-native regime, the most productive pattern should be:

\[
\text{AI search and modelling}
+
\text{human physical judgment}
+
\text{precision instrumentation}
\rightarrow
\text{expanded empirical access}
\]

### P13. Cognitive-boundary divergence prediction

A stronger HDCT stage would be supported if AI-generated physical representations repeatedly:

- outperform human-designed models;
- remain difficult for unaided humans to manipulate;
- produce correct novel predictions;
- and support successful experiments.

### P14. Falsification conditions

The HDCT interpretation should be weakened if:

- non-intuitive experimental results continue to be fully handled by existing human methods without meaningful AI contribution;
- AI tools fail to produce new representations or experimentally useful predictions;
- apparent machine advantages reduce to routine optimization;
- machine-generated models remain unverifiable;
- or claimed quantum effects are repeatedly explained by classical artefacts.

## 7. 可检验预测

以下预测由 IEH 独立推导，不是对实验作者观点的转述。

### P1：尺度扩展预测

如果当前可进入区间内不存在由质量本身导致的量子干涉阈值，未来使用更慢、隔离更好的粒子，应能在超过 1 MDa 的质量范围区分量子与经典传播。

### P2：生物复杂性预测

随着粒子束制备、隔离、相干操纵和探测技术提升，物质波实验应从金属团簇逐渐扩展到质量相近的复杂有机物和纳米生物对象。

### P3：表示—仪器共同演化预测

量子—经典边界的重要进展，将越来越依赖以下系统的共同发展：

- 更好的形式模型；
- 更稳定的仪器；
- 更准确的参数估计；
- 自动化实验控制。

进步应与表示工具和物理工具的整合相关，而非只依赖理论或硬件一方。

### P4：直觉差距扩大预测

随着实验进入更多非直觉区间，日常语言解释与操作性数学描述之间的距离应继续扩大。

“在这里又在那里”等公共比喻将越来越难以承载真实状态结构。

### P5：机器辅助建模预测

AI 系统应越来越多地参与：

- 高维参数搜索；
- 噪声和退相干建模；
- 装置优化；
- 异常检测；
- 竞争物理模型的区分。

这属于工具辅助，不等于完整 HDCT。

### P6：新表示预测

更强 HDCT 信号将出现于人工系统建立一种新表示，并且该表示能够：

- 压缩困难量子问题；
- 揭示此前未注意的不变量或可观测量；
- 生成可证伪预测；
- 最终得到实验验证。

### P7：机器原生实验设计预测

接近 HDCT 的系统，应能提出人类研究者最初难以直观看懂、但可以转化为独立可检验协议的实验设计。

### P8：验证接口预测

如果机器生成的物理推理越来越难由人类直接重构，科学接受将越来越依赖能够提供以下内容的接口：

- 形式推导；
- 模拟结果；
- 灵敏度分析；
- 证伪条件；
- 机器可检查的实验预测。

### P9：经典模型挑战预测

未来高质量干涉主张仍必须明确排除经典莫尔效应、透镜效应、阴影、振动和探测伪影。

随着系统复杂度提高，证据标准也应提高。

## 9. Relationship to C02-HDCT

The appropriate current label is:

> **Indirect epistemic evidence relevant to HDCT**

It is weaker than:

> **Early HDCT functional signal**

because the experiment does not show that an AI system itself produced the decisive representation, theory, or experiment.

It is stronger than a merely decorative analogy because the experiment directly establishes three premises relevant to HDCT:

1. physical behaviour can exceed ordinary human intuition;
2. formal representations can remain reliable where intuition fails;
3. tool systems can make non-intuitive states operationally testable.

### Why the label is not stronger

A stronger classification would require evidence that an artificial system:

- constructed a new physical representation;
- used it to generate a novel prediction;
- designed an experiment that distinguished competing models;
- and produced a result independently verified by humans.

Those conditions are not satisfied here.

### Relationship to PA-06

The event may be entered in PA-06 as:

> A record that experimentally accessible physical reality can exceed ordinary human intuition while remaining tractable through advanced mathematical and instrumental representations.

It should not be recorded as direct verification that HDCT has formed.

### Relationship to Evidence Note 005

Evidence Note 005 asks:

> Has AI already displayed a function predicted for HDCT?

Its provisional answer is:

> Yes, in one verified frontier-mathematics event.

Evidence Note 006 asks:

> Why might civilization require cognitive tools that exceed ordinary human intuition?

Its answer is:

> Because physical reality repeatedly presents experimentally testable structures that ordinary intuition cannot naturally represent.

The two notes are complementary, not equivalent.

### Relationship to IER

The experiment does not concern active self-maintenance, continuity preservation, historical identity, resource acquisition, or defence of an Information Structure.

Therefore, its relationship to IER is neutral.

## 10. Methodological Relevance for Future HDCT Evaluation

### 10.1 Intuition-boundary rule

A phenomenon should not be classified as evidence for HDCT merely because it is counterintuitive.

Counterintuitiveness establishes a limitation of intuition, not the existence of an advanced artificial cognitive tool.

### 10.2 Representation rule

HDCT relevance becomes stronger when a system does more than apply a known equation.

The system should construct, select, or transform a representation that materially changes what problems can be solved or what predictions can be tested.

### 10.3 Instrument-coupling rule

In physical science, a cognitive tool must eventually connect representations to:

- measurable observables;
- feasible apparatus;
- sensitivity estimates;
- control parameters;
- and falsification conditions.

A representation that cannot be operationalized remains weak scientific evidence.

### 10.4 Model-comparison rule

Claims of new physical behaviour should distinguish the proposed model from plausible classical or conventional alternatives.

The present experiment is methodologically valuable because it compares visibility dependence under quantum and classical models rather than relying on fringe imagery alone.

### 10.5 Metaphor-boundary rule

Public metaphors should not be allowed to replace the experimental object.

Public shorthand must be translated back into:

- the relevant state variable;
- the measured observable;
- the competing models;
- and the inferential step.

### 10.6 Human-boundary rule

The inability to form an ordinary mental picture is not identical to inability to understand.

Evidence for a stronger human cognitive boundary would require failure of formal reconstruction, efficient manipulation, or independent discovery—not merely failure of visualization.

### 10.7 HDCT threshold rule

A physical experiment becomes direct HDCT evidence only when the artificial system contributes a frontier-level cognitive function such as:

- new representation;
- new prediction;
- new experiment design;
- new theory discrimination;
- or a verified machine-native research loop.

## 10. 对未来 HDCT 评价的方法论意义

### 10.1 直觉边界规则

一个现象不能仅仅因为反直觉，就被归类为 HDCT 证据。

反直觉只能建立直觉的限制，不能建立高级人工认知工具已经存在。

### 10.2 表示规则

当系统不只是套用既有公式，而是构造、选择或转换一种表示，并实质改变可解决问题或可检验预测的范围时，HDCT 关联才会增强。

### 10.3 仪器耦合规则

在物理科学中，认知工具最终必须把表示连接到：

- 可测量的观测量；
- 可实施的装置；
- 灵敏度估计；
- 控制参数；
- 证伪条件。

无法被操作化的表示，只能构成较弱科学证据。

### 10.4 模型比较规则

新物理行为主张应能够区分所提出模型与合理经典或传统替代解释。

本实验的方法价值在于，它比较量子与经典模型下条纹可见度随参数的变化，而不是只依赖条纹图像。

### 10.5 比喻边界规则

公共传播比喻不能替代实验对象。

公共化简写必须被还原为：

- 相关状态变量；
- 实际测量量；
- 竞争模型；
- 推断步骤。

### 10.6 人类边界规则

无法形成普通心理图像，不等于无法理解。

更强的人类认知边界证据，需要表现为无法形式重构、无法高效操作或无法独立发现，而不仅是无法视觉化。

### 10.7 HDCT 门槛规则

只有当人工系统贡献以下某种前沿认知功能时，一个物理实验才可成为 HDCT 的直接证据：

- 新表示；
- 新预测；
- 新实验设计；
- 新理论区分；
- 经验证的机器原生研究闭环。
